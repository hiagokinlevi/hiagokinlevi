## Cyber Port: a path from first principles to research

Cyber Port maps **29 knowledge domains and 233 specializations**, with no fixed ceiling on new areas. Each specialization has plans for **nine course levels**: rapid, professional, technical, technologist, bachelor's, engineering, postgraduate, master's, and doctoral research. One principal study book is planned for each course. The book leads the learning path; playbooks support its chapters and labs from a separate folder within the topic.

The editorial order is **English → Portuguese → Spanish → Hebrew → French → Russian → German → Chinese → Japanese**. A finished book must meet the [publication standard](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/cyber-port/blob/main/BOOK_STANDARD.md): at least **300 verified A4 pages**, **50 exercises with worked answers**, practical examples, labs, and sources. Curricula and chapter plans are public, with English manuscript drafts underway. A plan or draft is not a completed book. Course labels do not claim accreditation or professional licensing.

### Research themes in DAI and ADAI

- **Authority and governance:** action boundaries, separate authorization, and accountable oversight.
- **Evidence and verification:** source provenance, independent checks, recomputable receipts, and reviewable decisions.
- **Defensive operations:** incident analysis, safe escalation, proposed containment, and explicit stop conditions.
- **Adversarial resilience:** hostile inputs, prompt injection, trust boundaries, and model-output limitations.
- **Evaluation:** assumptions, reported observations, reproducibility, and stated limits.

Read the primary [DAI paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23089652) and [ADAI paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23089594) for the complete definitions and claims.
