## CyberAB: learn from first principles to research

[CyberAB](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/CyberAB) organizes **23 academic areas in five schools** across **nine formation levels**. Each level has one course per area. Every course has one AI skill and one principal book planned for each of nine languages; each curricular subject becomes a book chapter with examples and exercises. This means **207 course designs** and **1,863 planned book-language editions**.

Start with the [level index](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/CyberAB/blob/main/levels/README.md), choose a course, then its language edition, [chapter index](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/CyberAB/blob/main/levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/en/SUBJECTS.md) and book. [Incident Response](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/CyberAB/blob/main/levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/README.md) is the first English writing priority. Its [rapid book](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/CyberAB/blob/main/levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) is a public draft; the planned books have **not** been presented as complete volumes.

## DAI and ADAI research

DAI studies the model and its governed architecture. ADAI sets out a proposed defensive-autonomy framework. Both focus on authority boundaries, evidence, independent verification, safe escalation and the limits of model output. Their papers and repositories contain the full definitions, assumptions and limitations; the research is distinct from CyberAB's course books.
