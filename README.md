## OpenCyber Academy: learn from first principles to research

[OpenCyber Academy](https://opencyber.academy/) is free to read without an account. Its [public repository](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/OpenCyberAcademy) organizes **23 academic areas in five schools** across **nine formation levels**: 207 course tracks, with one principal book per course and language edition. Nine languages are in scope, making 1,863 planned book-language editions. These are curriculum plans, not a claim that every lesson or translation is complete.

Start [by subject](https://opencyber.academy/subjects/) or [by level](https://opencyber.academy/levels/), choose a course and language, then follow its lessons and worked exercises. The [rapid Incident Response course](https://opencyber.academy/courses/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/?lang=en) is one English entry point. The [curriculum guide](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/OpenCyberAcademy/blob/main/COURSE_GUIDE.md) and [study path](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi/OpenCyberAcademy/blob/main/STUDY_PATH.md) provide the GitHub route. Each authored edition has one main `MANUSCRIPT.md`; subjects become modules, with weekly chapters and daily lessons as teaching is written. English is the reference language, followed by Portuguese and Spanish. A published outline or partial manuscript is not a completed course.

## DAI and ADAI research

DAI studies the model and its governed architecture. ADAI sets out a proposed defensive-autonomy framework. Both focus on authority boundaries, evidence, independent verification, safe escalation and the limits of model output. Their papers and repositories contain the full definitions, assumptions and limitations; the research is distinct from OpenCyber Academy's course books.
